## Charges that stuck
1. No costs, rent, tax status or sales appear anywhere, so no profit can be shown (9 jurors).
2. The case says "online, 5 languages", but the record shows a WhatsApp-and-weekend-stand business with no checkout or delivery (9 jurors).
3. No legal notices, terms of sale or privacy page, yet the site takes customers' photos and deposits (5 jurors).

## What the defense got right
1. The business is real and cheap to run: made to order, no stock, already producing and selling at a stand.
2. The gaps are fixable and the owner's files already name them; the 10 EUR mug sits below the 13.99-18 EUR competitors found, leaving room for margin.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. Build a one-page cost sheet this week: blanks, ink, laser time, box, labour and stand rent per product, plus a break-even number of orders per week.
2. Restate the case honestly as a stand-pickup, WhatsApp-order business with the site as a catalogue, and ask for a conditional, pre-launch verdict.
3. Put the basics in place: SIRET, tax and VAT status, legal notices, terms of sale, a photo privacy notice, and remove the invented crossed-out prices.

## The sentence
This court does not call the business bad; it cannot be judged as described. Stop presenting it as a finished online shop. Spend this week on costs and legal basics, then bring back real numbers from a few stand weekends. Until then, treat every profit claim as unproven.
